Normal Distribution

Manufacturing Quality Control

🏭 Industry Problem

A manufacturing company produces shafts with a target diameter of 10 mm. Due to small manufacturing variations, the actual diameter of each shaft is not exactly the same.

The quality-control team wants to estimate how many shafts will satisfy the specification limits.

Illustrative Example
Target diameter: 10 mm
Mean: μ = 10 mm
Standard deviation: σ = 0.05 mm
Acceptable range: 9.90 mm to 10.10 mm

🎓 Beginner Explanation

Normal Distribution: The bell-shaped curve represents how shaft diameters are expected to vary around the average diameter.

Specification limits: Shafts between the lower and upper limits are considered acceptable. Shafts outside these limits are treated as defective.

Why CDF? The Normal CDF tells us the probability that a shaft measurement is less than or equal to a particular value. Therefore, subtracting two CDF values gives the probability that the measurement falls between two specification limits.
